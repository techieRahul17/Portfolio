## The problem

Technical interviews are expensive on both sides of the table. The company spends senior
engineering hours on them; the candidate spends the whole thing being assessed by
whoever happened to be free that afternoon, against a bar that moves between
interviewers. Two candidates can give the same answer and walk away with different
scores, and nobody in the room can tell you why.

We wanted to attack both halves at once: make interviews cheaper to run, and make them
**consistent enough to defend**.

## What we built

InterVueX runs the interview end to end.

**Résumé-aware question generation.** Questions are generated from the candidate's own
résumé and then adapted live off their answers — so the interview follows the person in
front of you instead of a static list someone wrote last quarter.

**Live speech sentiment analysis.** Candidate speech is analysed during the interview,
giving the interviewer a read on how the conversation is actually going rather than a
guess reconstructed from memory afterwards.

**Rubric-based evaluation.** Every answer is scored against a defined rubric. This is
the part that makes the whole thing fair: the same response gets the same score whoever
is running the session.

**Practice mode.** Candidates can run interviews against the AI on their own and get
feedback — the same engine, pointed at preparation instead of assessment.

**Live video rooms.** Real-time video and audio over WebRTC, with running transcripts
and anti-cheating measures built into the session.

**Interview reports.** Everything above collapses into one report per candidate: the
transcript, the rubric scores, the sentiment read, and where the answers actually landed.

<Callout>
  The rubric is the load-bearing feature. AI-generated questions are a nice demo; scoring
  those answers the same way every single time is what makes the output usable as a
  hiring decision.
</Callout>

## Architecture

- **Frontend** — React with Tailwind CSS
- **Backend** — Node.js and Express
- **AI / NLP** — Python services for question generation and sentiment analysis
- **Database** — MongoDB
- **Real-time** — WebRTC for video and audio

Splitting the AI work into Python services rather than forcing it into the Node process
was deliberate: the NLP tooling lives in Python, and pretending otherwise would have
cost us the entire hackathon in glue code.

## What I worked on

I built the **responsive frontend** and owned the seam between it and the backend — which
on a real-time product is most of the difficulty. Specifically:

- the interview interface, and making it hold up during a live session
- frontend-to-backend integration for real-time interviews
- wiring in **live transcripts and speech sentiment analysis**, so the interviewer sees
  the read as the answer happens rather than after it
- the **video meeting features and anti-cheating logic**

The hard part was never a single feature. It was that video, transcript, sentiment and
scoring all had to stay in sync in one interface while a real person was mid-sentence.
Any one of them lagging makes the whole product feel broken.

## Result

InterVueX took **first place at the InnovateX Hackathon 2025**, judged on being
impactful, scalable and genuinely usable — a hiring tool that saves companies time while
making the experience better for the person being interviewed.

## What we learned

That AI is at its most useful here not as the thing that decides, but as the thing that
makes the decision *consistent*: generating the right question, capturing what was
actually said, and scoring it the same way every time. The interviewer still decides —
they just decide with better evidence, and the candidate gets a fairer, less stressful
hour.

## What I'd do differently

I'd put more design effort into how the rubric scores are surfaced during the interview.
We nailed capturing them; presenting them live without biasing the interviewer mid-answer
is a genuinely hard interface problem, and we solved it late.
